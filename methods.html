Use these maps to compare reported shock properties, then inspect the material state, loading conditions and source method behind each value.

01 / The axes

HEL and spall strength

Hugoniot elastic limit, HEL is the longitudinal stress at the elastic precursor under uniaxial strain. It marks the onset of inelastic response in the reported shock experiment and differs from quasistatic tensile yield strength.

Spall strength is the tensile failure stress inferred from the reported experiment. For velocity-pullback measurements, the result depends on the wave-speed choice and any waveform correction used by the authors.

Both axes use GPa on a logarithmic scale, so equal distances represent equal ratios. Reported zero strength is retained in the source records but cannot be placed on these axes.

02 / Evidence levels

Reported values, pairs and estimates

A registered paper may still be awaiting extraction. Values are included after checking the property, material condition, experimental method and location in the primary paper. Reused experiments, simulations and cited comparison data are identified separately.

Verified same-shot pair

HEL and spall strength belong to one identified shot, and the source table has been checked visually. These records appear in the verified-pair view.

XML-reviewed same-shot pair

The machine-readable version of the paper (XML) and its methods identify both stresses for one shot. The values are reported evidence; the PDF table still needs a visual check before they enter the verified-pair view.

Primary full-text review

Each value retains its extraction location, experimental method, source-file hash and visual-review status. Static properties may come from table cells or literal results prose, with a distinct review status for each. Reductions of measured wave histories or fracture loads remain experimental results. Pairing is reviewed separately.

Other reported coordinates

Both coordinates are reported, but may be independent values, condition summaries or ranges. Solid outlines identify clouds with reported coordinates; they do not establish same-shot pairing.

Matched or substituted

A missing coordinate has been supplied by a documented donor value. Clouds containing a match or substitution have dashed outlines.

Yield-based HEL

HEL has been estimated from yield strength using the conditional model below. Clouds containing this estimate have dotted outlines, including those that also contain donor matches.

The explorer’s “Reported coordinates only” filter rebuilds the clouds using records without inferred coordinates. The bounds and supporting-record counts change with the selected evidence.

03 / Cloud geometry

How material clouds are constructed

Records are grouped by exact material name. The independent minimum and maximum HEL and spall values define a bounding box. In log space, multiplying each half-span by √2 gives an ellipse that encloses all four corners.

r = ½ max(log₁₀(max) − log₁₀(min), 0.075) × √2

The minimum full span of 0.075 decades keeps single values and narrow ranges visible. Select a cloud to see its reported bounds and whether this minimum display width was applied.

Darker fill indicates more supporting extracted records relative to the largest material group. A summary is counted once, even when it describes several tests. Cloud area and fill therefore describe literature coverage rather than scatter, confidence or the number of specimens tested.

Colour identifies material class. Documented variants retain separate names and clouds, even when they share a colour family.

04 / Missing coordinates

How missing coordinates are handled

A configured completion rule may use a reported value from the same source and material state, another paper on the same material, or a documented state or composition substitute. Each inferred coordinate links to its donor and matching rationale. Only original reported values can serve as donors.

The optional yield-based estimate assumes isotropic elasticity, uniaxial strain and pressure-insensitive yielding:

HEL = Y × (1 − ν) / (1 − 2ν)

Y and HEL use the same stress unit. Applying this relation to quasistatic yield does not account for rate strengthening, pressure, temperature, anisotropy or precursor decay. Its assumptions may be unsuitable for polymers and glasses. Displayed endpoint ranges reflect the selected model inputs, not statistical uncertainty.

This model estimates HEL only. A record without a supported spall value remains unresolved.

05 / Comparability

Conditions needed for comparison

Compare grain size, texture, phase, heat treatment, temperature, loading method, pulse duration, peak stress and strain rate. A material cloud can span several such conditions; its interior does not represent observed combinations of HEL and spall.

Individual records retain the available test conditions and the authors’ uncertainty definitions. An unreported condition remains missing and should limit any quantitative comparison.

The reviewed classical map compares yield strength in MPa with K-based fracture toughness in MPa√m. Tensile strength, elongation and tensile work of fracture are separate properties. Conditional KQ, indentation toughness and elastic–plastic equivalent K retain their method families and validity qualifications. J-integral results keep their reported energy-per-area units; the atlas does not impose a new J-to-K conversion or treat an elastic K component as total toughness.

Source-review status and property pairing are separate. The static–dynamic comparison uses independent static and dynamic literature sets. Material clouds may span states or papers; class coverage may span alloys. Static condition points require reviewed experiment or condition links, including explicit links between separate tensile and fracture test populations. Yield-test kinds and toughness families remain separate. The multivariable analysis states which measured features enter each model.

06 / Published static context

Published static
reference ranges

The static–dynamic comparison includes published material-family ranges from Ashby’s 2021 Ansys/Granta booklet, Tables A3–A4 ↗. The source table cells were checked visually and retain row and page locators. The ranges combine independent strength and toughness bounds for each family.

Metal ranges use yield strength; polymer ranges retain the source’s yield/nonlinearity definition. Ceramic and glass ranges use tensile fracture strength. The broad reference view therefore labels its x axis “Strength”. George, Raabe & Ritchie (2019), Figure 2d ↗ provides chart context and a link to earlier sources. Its outlines were not extracted as new measurements.

07 / Coverage

What the coverage counts include

Clouds use records with positive, supported coordinates. The source library and CSV also retain unresolved values, reported zero strength, explicit no-spall outcomes and reviewed exclusions. A source-only entry is a registered paper without extracted dynamic records; it may still supply static properties or context. Counts distinguish reported same-shot rows, visually verified pairs, summaries and inferred coordinates. These categories can overlap. The collection is curated and incomplete.

01 / The axes

Shock yielding meets tensile failure.

Hugoniot elastic limit, HEL is the longitudinal stress associated with the elastic precursor and onset of inelastic response under shock loading. It is not identical to a uniaxial tensile yield strength.

Spall strength is the dynamic tensile stress associated with spall under the reported experiment and analysis. Pullback-based estimates can depend on the wave-speed choice and waveform correction.

Both axes use GPa on a logarithmic scale. Equal distances represent equal ratios. Reported zero spall is kept separately because zero has no finite logarithmic coordinate.

02 / Evidence levels

Keep measurements and inference distinct.

Valency supports paper discovery. Search hits and abstracts remain candidates until the primary publisher or author full text supports the value, exact material, experimental method and source locator. Duplicate reports, numerical models and cited comparison data do not become new experiments.

Verified same-shot pair

Both measured properties meet the admission rules and belong to the same experimental shot. Only these records appear in the verified-pair view.

XML-reviewed same-shot pair

A reviewed publication table row and its methods support a shared experimental shot and both measured stresses. These values contribute as reported evidence, but a pending PDF visual check keeps them separate from the verified-pair view.

Primary full-text review

New experimental values retain table-cell and method locators, the reviewed source-file digest and any PDF visual-check status. Experimental reductions of wave histories or fracture loads remain distinct from simulation predictions. Review alone does not establish a same-shot or matched-condition pair.

Other reported coordinates

Both properties are available without a completion model, but the evidence may be independent values, summary statistics or ranges. A solid cloud outline does not establish same-shot pairing.

Matched or substituted

At least one coordinate uses an explicitly configured donor. Dashed cloud outlines identify a cloud containing this kind of inference.

Yield-based HEL

At least one HEL coordinate uses a conditional yield conversion. Dotted outlines take precedence over other outline categories.

Use the explorer’s “Reported coordinates only” filter to rebuild clouds from records with no inferred coordinate. This changes the bounds and record counts; it does not merely hide the outline.

03 / Cloud geometry

An enclosure, not a probability.

Each exact named material pools its eligible original records. Minimum and maximum HEL and spall coordinates form an independent bounding box. In log space, the ellipse radii are the half-spans multiplied by √2 so that all four box corners are enclosed.

r = ½ max(log₁₀(max) − log₁₀(min), 0.075) × √2

The minimum full span of 0.075 decades prevents single values and narrow ranges from disappearing. That added width is a rendering convention, not measured scatter. Selected clouds state whether this padding was applied.

Fill opacity scales with the number of original extracted records relative to the largest material group. It does not represent experimental population size, probability density or confidence. One extracted summary record is counted once even if the paper describes a larger experimental campaign.

Materials share their class hue. Documented material variants may share a colour family while retaining separate clouds and exact material names.

04 / Missing coordinates

Complete carefully. Leave gaps honestly.

Completion rules are explicit, reviewed entries in the configuration. They can use a same-source material-state match, a cross-paper match for the same material, or a documented material-state or composition substitute. Donors supply original reported values; inferred values cannot recursively become donors.

Where configured, isotropic uniaxial-strain elasticity with a pressure-insensitive yield criterion gives:

HEL = Y × (1 − ν) / (1 − 2ν)

Y and HEL must use the same stress unit. Quasistatic yield strength is not shock-rate yield strength. The model does not correct for strain rate, pressure, temperature, anisotropy or precursor decay; polymers and glasses can violate its assumptions. Its endpoint ranges are input-sensitivity bounds, not statistical uncertainty.

The model cannot provide missing spall strength. If no defensible spall counterpart exists, the record remains unresolved.

05 / Comparability

A material name is not the whole experiment.

Grain size, texture, phase, heat treatment, temperature, pulse duration, loading method, peak stress and strain rate can all matter. A cloud pools coverage across recorded states; it does not claim that every combination inside the ellipse is physically observed.

Inspect the material state and test conditions on individual records before making a quantitative comparison. Missing conditions are shown as unreported. Original uncertainty definitions remain attached to each record.

The reviewed classical map uses yield strength in MPa and K-based fracture toughness in MPa√m. Tensile strength, fracture elongation and energy absorbed in an unnotched tensile test do not substitute for those properties. Conditional KQ, indentation toughness and elastic–plastic equivalent K retain separate method families and validity qualifications. J-integral toughness remains in its reported energy-per-area units; no new J-to-K conversion is imposed. An elastic K component used within a J calculation is not the total toughness.

New full-text reviews preserve their own source status. They do not automatically become visually verified same-shot pairs. The static–dynamic comparison starts with independent material clouds: reported property extents may come from different states or papers. Static yield-test kinds and toughness families remain separate. Class coverage can span different alloys; only the optional measured-pair view requires reviewed experiment or condition links. The two maps do not require matching materials. The structure explorer states which measured features enter each model.

06 / Published static context

Broader coverage.
Explicit strength definitions.

The static–dynamic comparison also offers 43 published material-family ranges from Ashby’s 2021 Ansys/Granta booklet, Tables A3–A4 ↗. Table cells were visually reviewed and retain their source, row and page. These reference envelopes show independent strength and toughness bounds; their area is not a measured joint distribution.

Metal ranges use yield strength. Polymer ranges retain the source’s yield/nonlinearity definition. Ceramic and glass ranges use explicitly reported tensile fracture strength, so the broad view labels its x axis as strength rather than universal yield. George, Raabe & Ritchie (2019), Figure 2d ↗ was reviewed for chart context and its adapted-source lineage; its outlines were not digitized into new measurements.

In the static–dynamic comparison, choose Reviewed measurements under Static dataset to inspect the experimental input alone. Published ranges add no measured values or experimental source counts, do not supply HEL estimates, and do not enter rankings or embeddings. Their fill does not encode a measurement count. 07 / Coverage

Every record has a place in the ledger.

Positive supported records contribute to clouds. Unresolved records, reported zero spall and reviewed exclusions remain searchable in the source library and downloadable CSV. Source-only entries track registered papers with no extracted evidence yet.

The build verifies record uniqueness, source links and cloud membership, and rejects local-study identifiers and machine paths in the web payload. See the repository’s extraction protocol ↗ and figure guide ↗ for the implementation rules.
